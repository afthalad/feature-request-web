# Fewchurs — Brand Psychology & Logo Brief

A senior-designer read of the product, the audience, and the current brand, written to be handed to an image/vector AI tool (Recraft, Ideogram, Midjourney, etc.) together with your moodboard.

---

## 1. The brand in one sentence

> **Fewchurs turns many scattered voices into the few things worth building — and tells everyone when it ships.**

The whole identity should express one movement: **many → few → done.**
Noise converges into a clear signal, and the signal becomes something delivered.

A detail worth building the brand on: the name literally starts with **"few."** Out of hundreds of requests, the product helps a developer find the *few* features that matter. That is not an accident to hide — it's the story.

---

## 2. What the product actually is (beneath the features)

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| Surface | A feature-request board + SDK for apps: submit, vote, comment, follow, status, email on ship. |
| Job to be done | "Help me decide what to build next without guessing, and without a support inbox." |
| Emotional job (developer) | Relief from doubt. Confidence that the next month of work won't be wasted. |
| Emotional job (end user) | Being heard. Seeing proof that asking actually changed something. |
| The magic moment | The "it's Done" email. It closes the loop, and it's the day users reopen the app. |

**The core loop — the brand should feel like this loop:**

1. **Collect** — ideas land in one place (many, scattered, inward motion)
2. **Rank** — votes settle the argument (order, fairness, rising)
3. **Decide** — you mark what you're doing (clarity, commitment)
4. **Close** — they hear back when it ships (spark, delight, completion)

Visual translation: **inward convergence → upward rise → a small spark of completion.**

## 3. Audience psychology

### Primary: the indie developer
Solo founders, weekend app builders, contractors shipping for clients. Mostly iOS, but multi-platform.

- **Works alone**, so every week spent on the wrong feature hurts personally.
- **Fear:** building something nobody wanted. **Desire:** certainty without doing user research.
- **Allergic to enterprise SaaS** — sales calls, seat pricing, dashboards full of charts. Your pricing ($0 / $5 / $9) and copy already reflect this.
- **Has taste.** Uses tools like Linear, Raycast, Things, Arc. They judge a product by its craft in the first three seconds. A cheap-looking logo reads as "this SDK will be buggy."
- **Wants to feel like a real company** without becoming one. The brand should make a solo dev look more polished inside their own app.

### Secondary: the dev's end users
Casual app users who tap "Suggest a feature." They never sign up, never learn the name.

- They see the board, the "Powered by Fewchurs" badge on Free, and the email.
- The mark must be **friendly, non-technical, and unintimidating** at tiny sizes inside someone else's app.
- It must **never overpower the host app's brand** — the product's whole promise is "a board that looks like your app."

**Design implication:** the symbol must work *quietly* (a 12–16px badge inside another brand) and *confidently* (a 1024px app icon / social avatar). Two very different jobs — simplicity is non-negotiable.

## 4. Audit of the current brand

### What's working — keep it
- **The voice is the best asset you have.** "The loudest person stops winning." "Three lines of code and I had a board. That's the entire pitch, and it's true." Plain, candid, slightly dry, anti-hype. The visual identity should sound like this copy.
- **Warm orange primary.** The feedback/roadmap category is dominated by blue and purple SaaS. Orange is ownable, warm, and action-oriented.
- **Be Vietnam Pro** is a good, friendly-but-precise UI face. Keep it for product and headlines.
- **Warm neutrals** (near-black with a slight warm tint rather than pure grey) — mature, not sterile.

### What's broken — fix it
1. **The logo is a placeholder.** An "F" in a rounded square is what every unbranded project uses. It carries no meaning and is not ownable or trademarkable.
2. **The favicon contradicts the brand.** `src/app/icon.svg` is **indigo `#4F39D6` with Arial**, while the whole product is **orange** with Be Vietnam Pro. Browser tabs currently show a different brand than the site.
3. **The tagline "get rid of guessings" has a grammar problem.** "Guessings" isn't standard English; native speakers read it as a typo, which quietly costs trust with a developer audience. The name is already an intentional misspelling — a second quirk dilutes the first one. Alternatives in the same voice:
   - *Stop guessing what to build.*
   - *Build what they asked for.*
   - *Less guessing. More shipping.*
   - *Your users already know what's next.*
4. **Earlier logo concepts from this session were rough placeholders** (thin strokes, generic arrows and speech bubbles). Don't use them as a base — they're below the level of your references.

### The name: "Fewchurs"
- **Strengths:** phonetic spelling of "features" — playful, indie, memorable, and uniquely searchable (like Flickr, Lyft, Tumblr). Contains "few," which carries the brand idea.
- **Risk:** when heard aloud, people won't know how to spell it. Mitigate with a strong, clear wordmark and consistent use of the domain.
- **Wordmark rule:** always lowercase `fewchurs`. Lowercase signals approachable and indie; title case makes the misspelling look accidental.

## 6. Reading your moodboard

You shared five references. Their shared DNA — this is what you're responding to:

1. **One flat white mark on a saturated field.** No outlines, no multi-color mark.
2. **Construction from circles and quarter-arcs.** Every curve looks drawn with a compass.
3. **Negative space does the storytelling** — the star, the lens, the gap in the "S."
4. **Heavy weight, solid mass.** Nothing is a thin line. Reads at small sizes.
5. **Symmetry or rotational balance** — calm, stable, trustworthy.
6. **Atmospheric backgrounds** (grain, halftone, soft gradient, dark vignette) — but **the mark itself stays flat.**
7. **A spark / four-point star** appears in three of five — a shared signal of "moment of value."

**How each reference maps to Fewchurs:**

| Reference | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Four inward arrows forming a star | **Strongest conceptual fit** | It's literally the product: many inputs converge into one clear thing. Use the *idea*, not the drawing — that exact mark belongs to someone else. |
| Concave four-point star | Strong | The "spark of shipping." Good as a secondary element or animation end-state. |
| "S" from quarter-rounded blocks | Strong (construction) | Great model for building a lowercase **f** from quarter shapes. |
| "miracle" wordmark + sparkle | Good (wordmark tone) | Right lowercase, rounded, friendly-tech feel. Don't copy the sparkle-after-word layout. |
| Two overlapping circles | **Avoid** | Reads instantly as Mastercard. Also too generic to own. |

> **Originality note:** use these as *mood and construction* references only. Brief AI tools with the concept and rules below — not "make it look like this image" — and run a trademark/visual search on the finalist.

## 7. Visual psychology — why these choices

### Shape
| Form | Psychological read | Use for Fewchurs |
|---|---|---|
| Circle / quarter-arc | Community, inclusion, friendliness | Base construction — every shape derived from circles |
| Rotational symmetry (4 identical parts) | Fairness, equality, balance | "Every voice has equal weight" |
| Inward-pointing forms | Focus, gathering, collection | Requests landing in one place |
| Upward direction | Progress, ranking, growth | Votes rising, roadmap moving |
| Four-point star in negative space | A spark, delight, "done" | The ship moment, the email |
| Rounded terminals | Approachable, safe, human | Softens the geometry for non-technical end users |
| Sharp inner points | Precision, decisiveness | Keeps it from feeling soft or vague |

**The tension to hold:** soft outer silhouette (friendly) + crisp inner negative space (decisive). That combination is what makes a mark feel both human and competent.

### Color
**Orange** reads as energy, warmth, optimism, action, approachability. It's the color of "let's do it," not "let's schedule a meeting." It's also a sharp differentiator in a blue/purple category.

- Risk: orange drifting toward red reads as warning/error. Your hue (~42° in OKLCH) is safely orange — keep it there, don't push redder.
- Risk: too much orange is tiring. Use it as the **icon field and accent**, not as a page background.

### Typography
Rounded-geometric lowercase = friendly and modern. Monoline weight with slightly squared-off curves = engineered. Avoid script, serif, or ultra-thin weights.

### Motion (for a logo animation / loading state)
Four shapes glide in from the corners, lock together, and the negative-space star flashes once.
~600ms, ease-out, one small overshoot. It dramatizes the product loop: **gather → lock in → ship.**
The same motion works as the SDK's "request submitted" confirmation.

## 8. Logo directions

Four directions, ranked. **Recommended system: Direction A (symbol) + Direction D (wordmark).**

### Direction A — "Convergence" (recommended symbol)
**Concept:** Four identical rounded shapes, rotated 90° around a center, each pointing inward. The gap between them forms a crisp **four-point star** in negative space.

- **Story:** many voices (four equal parts) converge into one clear thing (the star) — and the star is also the spark of shipping.
- **Why it works:** encodes fairness (symmetry), focus (convergence), and delight (spark) in one silhouette. Scales to 16px because it's four big masses.
- **Construction:** square grid; each part built from a quarter-circle + a rectangle; outer corners fully rounded; inner edges concave arcs meeting in sharp star points. Optional: a subtle inward notch/chevron on each part to hint "arrow" — keep it bold or leave it out.
- **Risk:** four-part stars are a popular form. Differentiate through the specific shape of each part (e.g. each piece is a lowercase "f"-like hook, or a rounded upvote chevron).

### Direction B — "The Few"
**Concept:** A small grid of dots (the many requests) where the dots are arranged or sized so an **upward chevron** emerges — the few that rise.

- **Story:** literal "few from many"; votes stacking into a direction.
- **Why it works:** echoes the halftone texture in your moodboard; very ownable; great for animation (dots rising into place).
- **Construction:** 3×3 or 4×4 grid of perfect circles; the chevron is formed by larger dots or by removing dots. Single color.
- **Risk:** dot grids get muddy below 24px. Needs a simplified favicon version (just the chevron dots).

### Direction C — "Quarter f"
**Concept:** A lowercase **f** built entirely from quarter-circles and rounded rectangles (like your "S" reference). The hook of the f and its crossbar leave an **upward caret** in negative space.

- **Story:** the initial of the brand with an upvote hidden inside it.
- **Why it works:** direct brand recognition + a secret you discover on second look (people love that).
- **Construction:** 2×3 module grid; bowl = quarter-circle; stem = rounded rectangle; the gap between hook and crossbar shaped as a caret.
- **Risk:** letter monograms are harder to own; the caret must be obvious enough to matter.

### Direction D — "fewchurs" wordmark (recommended wordmark)
**Concept:** Custom lowercase geometric wordmark. The **w** is drawn so its middle apex rises slightly above the x-height into a soft **upvote caret** — a hidden vote inside the name.

- **Why it works:** makes the unusual spelling feel intentional and designed; the caret quietly ties to the product.
- **Construction:** start from a rounded geometric sans skeleton (see §10), heavy-medium weight, generous letter-spacing at small sizes, tight at display sizes; rounded terminals; single-story shapes.
- **Risk:** too subtle a caret disappears; too big and it looks like a glitch. Aim for the middle apex extending ~15–20% above x-height.

### Directions to avoid (category clichés)
- Speech bubbles (every feedback tool)
- Lightbulbs (every "ideas" product ever)
- Thumbs up / hearts
- Generic upvote arrow in a rounded square
- Overlapping circles (Mastercard)
- Gradients, 3D, glass, glows inside the mark
- Thin outline/stroke icons

## 9. Logo technical rules

- **One color only.** Must work solid white on orange, solid ink on white, solid white on ink.
- **No gradients, strokes, shadows, or textures in the mark.** Atmosphere goes in the background, never the symbol.
- **Built on a grid** from circles, quarter-circles, and rectangles. Curves must be geometric, not hand-wobbly.
- **Minimum size:** symbol legible at **16px**; wordmark legible at **14px** cap height.
- **Clear space:** at least ¼ of the symbol's width on all sides.
- **Optical center:** symbol visually centered in a square (nudge slightly up if it has an upward direction).
- **App icon:** symbol at ~55–60% of the icon width on an orange field, tested with the iOS rounded-rect mask.
- **Lockups:** horizontal (symbol + wordmark), stacked, symbol-only, wordmark-only.

**Deliverables**
- `logo-symbol.svg`, `logo-wordmark.svg`, `logo-horizontal.svg`, `logo-stacked.svg` (each in orange / ink / white)
- `favicon.svg` + 32px / 16px PNG (simplified if needed)
- `apple-touch-icon.png` 180×180, app icon 1024×1024
- Social avatar 400×400, OG image 1200×630
- "Powered by Fewchurs" badge (small, neutral, doesn't fight the host app)

## 10. Color & type system

### Colors (taken from your live design tokens)
| Role | Name | HEX | OKLCH | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Fewchurs Orange | `#ED5F18` | `0.66 0.19 42` | Icon field, accents, primary CTA |
| Primary strong | Ember | `#D14300` | `0.58 0.19 40` | Pressed state, small text on white |
| Primary soft | Apricot Mist | `#FFF2E8` | `0.97 0.02 60` | Tinted backgrounds, badges |
| Ink | Warm Ink | `#171614` | `0.20 0.004 60` | Dark surfaces, dark logo version |
| Text | Charcoal | `#1C1A18` | `0.22 0.005 60` | Body text |
| Muted | Stone | `#6C6865` | `0.52 0.006 60` | Secondary text |
| Base | White | `#FFFFFF` | `1 0 0` | Page background |
| Dark-mode primary | Glow Orange | `#F77036` | `0.70 0.18 42` | Orange on dark surfaces |

**Contrast facts (check before using):**
- White on `#ED5F18` = **3.36:1** → fine for the logo, icons, and large text; **not** for small button labels.
- White on `#D14300` = **4.65:1** → passes AA for normal text. Use this for small white-on-orange text.
- Ink `#171614` on `#ED5F18` = **5.39:1** → a dark mark on orange is actually *more* legible than white. Worth testing both.

**Proportion guide:** ~70% neutrals (white/warm greys), ~20% ink/charcoal, ~10% orange. Orange is the spark, not the wallpaper.

**Brand backgrounds (marketing, social, app store — never inside the mark):** a soft radial from `#ED5F18` to `#D14300` with a dark warm vignette in one corner, optionally with fine grain or halftone dots — matching the atmosphere of your references.

### Typography
- **Product + headlines:** Be Vietnam Pro (already in use). Bold for headlines, Medium for UI.
- **Code:** Geist Mono (already in use).
- **Wordmark:** custom-drawn. Good skeletons to start from: *Outfit, Sora, Plus Jakarta Sans, Manrope, General Sans, Satoshi.* Modify terminals and the "w" — never ship a font as-is for the wordmark.

## 11. Do / Don't

**Do**
- Keep the mark flat, single-color, and bold
- Build every curve from circles
- Let negative space carry the meaning
- Keep lowercase `fewchurs`
- Make the brand as plain-spoken as the copy

**Don't**
- Add a second accent color to the mark
- Use thin lines or outline icons for the logo
- Use speech bubbles, lightbulbs, or thumbs
- Push the orange toward red
- Put gradients or effects inside the symbol
- Let the badge compete with the host app's brand

## 13. How to judge the AI outputs

Score each candidate — keep only those that pass all of the first five.

1. **16px test** — shrink it to favicon size. Still recognizable?
2. **One-color test** — solid black on white. Still works?
3. **Squint test** — blur your eyes. Is the silhouette distinct?
4. **Describe-it test** — can someone describe it in five words after one second? ("four pieces making a star")
5. **Look-alike test** — reverse image search. Does it resemble Mastercard, Google Photos, Slack, Airtable, or an existing star-mark logo?
6. **Story test** — can you explain the meaning in one sentence tied to *many → few → done*?
7. **Host-app test** — place it small in a "Powered by" badge inside a random app screenshot. Does it stay polite?
8. **App icon test** — on the iOS rounded mask, next to other icons on a home screen. Does it hold its own?
9. **Wordmark pairing** — does the symbol's weight match the wordmark's stroke weight?
10. **Animation test** — can the parts plausibly move into place (gather → lock → spark)?
